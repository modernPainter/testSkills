# Interpreting Placement Quality Metrics

Metrics explain and compare acquired placement facts; they do not replace independent hard checks. Identify each metric's objects, source, units, coverage, and proxy status.

## Poses and fixed conditions

Compare positions, rotations, sides, and displacement with this task's input. Preserve native Lock and conversational fixed requirements; equal component counts are insufficient. Report discrepancies between actual writes and the selected candidate rather than trusting a success summary.

## Geometry and engineering quality

Actual out-of-board geometry, body collisions, and applicable courtyard/clearance issues belong to their respective hard checks. Establish what a quality summary's overlap/outside counts measure. Zero summary counts do not prove coverage of actual board shape, pads, or assembly envelopes.

Connection length, crossings, crowding, and area utilization are pre-route comparison proxies, not routing proof. Evaluate functional grouping, peripheral proximity, interface direction, grids, and arrangement under applicable project conditions; preferences do not become universal hard rules.

Without modules, relationships, or actual rules, missing measurements and default scores cannot establish correctness. Candidate comparison requires the same input, rules, grouping, criteria, and units. List incomparable dimensions.

Evaluate new candidates with the same functional groups and peripheral relationships acquired for this task and retain their sources. Without module information, mark the module dimension unmeasured even if the assessment reports a perfect score. Overall scores with and without module inputs cannot quantify placement improvement. Recorded grouping does not establish design intent; distinguish inference from project evidence.

## Quality, time, and tradeoffs

Meet hard conditions first, then list quality components, engineering requirements, and available timings. High scores cannot offset safety issues. Faster failure is not an efficiency advantage. Record traceable measurements; one short run does not establish stability.

Evidence-backed engineering tradeoffs may lower the total score. Identify the improved relationship, regressed metrics, and selection reason. Preserve evaluation criteria and grouping. Report missing measurements, unverified items, and confirmed risks separately.
